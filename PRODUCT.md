# Product

## Register

product

## Users

People who want to browse without unexpectedly encountering spoilers, distressing subjects, or other unwanted content. They use Spoilt in short, practical moments: configuring boundaries in the options page, checking protection status in the popup, and returning to normal browsing with minimal interruption.

## Product Purpose

Spoilt gives people calm, local control over what reaches their attention. It combines direct rules, local AI when available, and spoiler memory to conceal unwanted text and images before they are consumed. Success means the user can establish healthy boundaries, understand the current protection state, and trust the extension without feeling watched or alarmed.

## Brand Personality

Calm, protective, and tactile. The product should feel lighter than a security utility, orderly like Proton's product interfaces, and grounded in familiar physical materials such as paper, correction tape, and marker.

## Anti-references

- Codex-template styling: generic card grids, decorative gradients, oversized marketing typography, repetitive eyebrow labels, and other visibly generated UI conventions.
- "Brutalist" blacks: harsh black-dominated surfaces, square high-contrast blocks, and aggressive warning-sign aesthetics.
- Generic SaaS settings pages that make a personal boundary tool feel corporate, abstract, or emotionally cold.

## Design Principles

1. **Boundaries without alarm.** Protection should feel healthy and composed, not punitive, secretive, or dangerous.
2. **Make status immediately legible.** The popup must communicate whether protection is active and what it has done without requiring interpretation.
3. **Use familiar material cues.** Paper, correction tape, and marker redaction can make concealment understandable, but material effects must support the task rather than become decoration.
4. **Keep controls orderly and breathable.** Follow Proton-like discipline in spacing, moderate corner radii, grouping, and predictable interaction patterns.
5. **Preserve user trust.** Explain local AI, memory, web lookups, errors, and destructive actions in direct language at the point where they matter.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Follow current accessibility best practices: semantic HTML, full keyboard operation, visible focus states, sufficient text and control contrast, generous targets, reduced-motion support, and state communication that never relies on color alone. Redaction treatments must remain understandable across light, dark, textured, and image-heavy host pages.
